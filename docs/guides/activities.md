# Curios, trade, and the things to do between chests

Chests are the economy. Everything here is what the canyon is like when the Keeper is
Quiet, when your bag is full and the vault is a long walk away, or when you simply want
something to do that is not walking into skeletons.

None of it is a second income. The whole NPC economy pays out **less than twenty GOLD a
day across every player in the world**, and it is capped in four separate ways so that it
stays that way. It is a flavour economy — a way to turn a hide into a seed cake and an
oddment into a title — and the design is deliberate: **chests remain the only real source
of gold.**

## Curios

Thirty-two oddments the halls give up: poems, dolls, ledger pages, a bell clapper, things
with no use whatsoever. They are **not bag items**. Each one is a bit in your catalogue and
a bit in your satchel, so the whole collection costs sixteen bytes and cannot be dropped,
stolen, lost on death, or traded to another player.

They come out of item chests, at a rate that rises with depth — never on the surface, with
one exception below. The roll is **an extra, not a replacement**: a chest that gives you a
curio still gives you its item.

You never get a duplicate while anything is missing. Once you have all thirty-two, a chest
that would have given one gives **three bone dust** instead, and says so.

**An unfound curio shows its slot and never its words.** The catalogue displays thirty-two
silhouettes and fills them in as you find them.

### The Collector

**Quill Amberly** stands by the Lantern Inn. Bring her a curio and she pays **ten bone dust
per tier** — ten, twenty or thirty — and a point of karma, capped at five a turn so a
hoard handed over in one go does not buy a reputation.

Giving one away clears it from your satchel but **not from your catalogue**. What you found
stays found; that is why she can never be farmed for the same rare curio twice.

Complete a set and she pays **fifty bone dust**, two more karma, and gives you a name. There
are five sets: the Ballad, the Dolls, the Cache, the Bell, and the Flame. Each is paid once.

## Trade

Seven of the canyon's people keep a table, and they will swap what they have for what you
have. **Coin from an NPC arrives in your pouch, not your purse** — it is not yours until you
bank it at the vault, exactly like chest gold.

Four caps make this a flavour economy rather than a farm:

| the cap | what it bounds |
|---|---|
| each person's purse | **0.9 GOLD a turn**, then they buy nothing more |
| your earnings | **1.2 GOLD a turn** from all the folk together |
| your trades | **20 a turn** |
| per-offer stock | most rows run out before the purse does |

There is also a rule you will never see, which is the reason the caps hold: **no NPC will
ever pay at or above the Store's price for anything the Store sells.** That gap is checked
by a test, and a new offer that closed it would fail to build rather than quietly opening a
loop from gold to goods and back to gold.

### The two wandering buyers

**Wren the Tinker** and **Odd Bartholomew** walk the roads. They pay **fifteen GOLD for a
dragon scale** — far above anything else in the world — and they are the one exception to
the earnings cap.

What bounds them instead is the purse: **fifteen GOLD each per week**, and **one sale per
player per week** across both of them. So the world-wide outflow is thirty GOLD a week no
matter how many people go looking, and the number is a prize for knowing the market rather
than a wage.

They move every epoch and never stay in the same place twice running. The Keeper's Board
reports where each was **last seen by somebody** — not where it is now. A stale sighting is
the point: it sends you looking.

## Fishing

A cane rod from the Store, two hundred casts before it wants mending. Cast from the shore
at the Harbour, or in the flooded nave of the Drowned Chapel, standing on sand or path with
water in reach.

It never yields gold. What comes up is reagents, the occasional useful oddment, a riverfish
worth eight health — and, rarely, **a bottle with a curl of paper inside**, which is the
only source of curios above ground.

Marrow Quill mends a rod for **0.30 GOLD**; the Store charges twice that and two bone dust.

## The ferry and the inn

**Old Corliss** keeps a boat between the Harbour and the old landing under Hallowmere.
**0.30 GOLD** to cross, and it is quicker than the road.

**The Lantern Inn** rents a bed for **0.15 GOLD**. You mend faster for a while afterwards —
wounds close after two quiet ticks instead of five.

Both fares go to the treasury the NPC economy pays out of, which is the loop: what the
canyon pays you for hides and oddments is funded by what you pay it for passage and beds.

## The Ossuary

A low room of stacked bone beneath the Crypt, dry and very quiet. The door reads: *for
those who hold a tombstone.*

TOMBSTONE is only minted when the world has had a hard epoch and someone buried gold in the
Crypt, so until that happens **this room has no visitors at all**. It holds one chest, and
that chest opens only while the Keeper is Dark.

The door reads your balance and remembers the answer for an epoch, so entering is instant
rather than a wait. If it has not looked lately it will say so, and you knock.
